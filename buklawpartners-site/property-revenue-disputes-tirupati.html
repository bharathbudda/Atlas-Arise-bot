Property and Revenue Disputes in the Tirupati Region

Land is valuable and records are complex, which is why property and revenue disputes are so common across Tirupati and Chittoor District. A dispute may be over who owns the land, whose name should be on the patta, whether a mutation was correctly recorded, where a boundary lies, or whether someone has encroached on or grabbed your property. These disputes turn on documents, revenue records, and the correct forum, and resolving them requires both litigation skill and a deep understanding of AP revenue law.

We handle property and revenue disputes before the civil courts, the High Court of Andhra Pradesh, and the revenue authorities (RDO, MRO, Joint Collector), depending on the nature of the matter.

Protecting Possession

In property disputes, possession is critical. If someone is interfering with your land or threatening to dispossess you, an early injunction can protect your position while the dispute is resolved. Delay often makes matters harder to fix.

Why Revenue Records Matter

Many property disputes in AP are really disputes about records, a patta in the wrong name, a mutation that was never done, a survey subdivision that does not match the sale deed. We read the adangal, pattadar passbook, encumbrance certificate, and survey records together, so the dispute is fought on a complete and accurate picture rather than a single document.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do if someone encroaches on my land?

An injunction can be sought to protect your possession and prevent further encroachment, and pursue a suit for declaration and recovery of possession. In cases of land grabbing, proceedings under the AP Land Grabbing (Prohibition) Act may be available.

The patta is not in my name, what can I do?

If the patta or pattadar passbook does not reflect the correct owner, you can seek correction through the revenue authorities and, where necessary, through the civil court. We examine why the records are inconsistent and pursue the right remedy, whether it is mutation, correction, or a declaration of title.

What is a mutation dispute?

Mutation is the updating of revenue records to reflect a change of ownership. A mutation dispute arises when the records are not updated after a sale, or are updated in favour of the wrong person. Because revenue records and registration records must align, resolving mutation disputes is often key to protecting title.

How do I resolve a boundary or survey number dispute?

Boundary and survey disputes are resolved by examining the Field Measurement Book (FMB), survey records, and the sale deeds, and where necessary through a survey and a suit for declaration and injunction. We build the case on the survey evidence and the documents.

Which court handles property disputes in Tirupati?

It depends on the nature of the dispute, title and possession suits go to the civil courts, certain revenue matters go to the RDO, MRO, or Joint Collector, and some matters reach the High Court of Andhra Pradesh. We identify the correct forum and pursue the matter there.
