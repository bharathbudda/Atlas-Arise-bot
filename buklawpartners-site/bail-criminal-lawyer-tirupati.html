Criminal Defence and Bail

A criminal case is one of the most stressful situations anyone can face. The steps taken at the outset (bail, protection of rights during investigation, and the framing of the defence) can shape the entire course of the matter. The firm's advocates appear at every stage of a criminal proceeding, from arrest and bail through trial and appeal.

Bail Advocacy Is About Preparation

Bail is rarely automatic. It depends on how the facts are presented, the nature of the allegations, and the strength of the legal argument. A well-prepared bail application anticipates the prosecution's objections and addresses the court's real concerns: the risk of the accused absconding, tampering with evidence, or influencing witnesses. We prepare every bail matter with that standard in mind.

If You Anticipate Arrest

A person who has reason to believe they may be arrested in connection with a non-bailable offence can apply for anticipatory bail before arrest. The application lies to the Court of Session or the High Court under Section 482 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (formerly Section 438 of the Code of Criminal Procedure).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between anticipatory bail and regular bail?

Anticipatory bail is sought before arrest, when you apprehend being implicated in a case, and protects you against being taken into custody. Regular bail is sought after arrest, to secure release from custody while the case proceeds. Which one applies depends on whether the person has already been arrested.

Is there a time limit for applying for bail?

There is no fixed time limit, but bail matters are time-sensitive. An application needs the FIR and the details of the case, and the court considers factors such as the nature of the allegations, the risk of absconding, and the possibility of interference with evidence or witnesses.

Can an FIR be quashed?

In appropriate cases, yes. Where an FIR or a criminal proceeding is legally unsustainable, discloses no offence, or amounts to an abuse of the legal process, it can be challenged through a quashing petition before the High Court under its inherent powers (now Section 528 of the BNSS, formerly Section 482 of the Code of Criminal Procedure).

Do you handle criminal appeals?

Yes. We handle criminal appeals and revisions against conviction and sentence before the appellate courts and the High Court of Andhra Pradesh, examining the trial record for errors of fact and law that support the appeal.
