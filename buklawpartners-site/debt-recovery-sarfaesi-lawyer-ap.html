Debt Recovery and Banking Law in Andhra Pradesh

When a bank or financial institution moves to enforce a security, or when a borrower needs to challenge that enforcement, the matter is governed by a tight procedural framework with strict timelines. The two principal statutes are the SARFAESI Act, which allows secured creditors to enforce security without the intervention of a court, and the Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, under which the Debt Recovery Tribunal hears recovery applications and securitisation applications. Understanding the interplay between these, and acting within the limitation periods, is what determines the outcome.

The firm acts for borrowers, guarantors, and other affected parties in these matters before the Debt Recovery Tribunal at Hyderabad and, where relevant, the National Company Law Tribunal and the civil courts.

Matters in This Area

Securitisation Applications (SA)

Recovery Applications (OA)

IBC, Section 7 & Section 9

Insolvency matters before the NCLT, including applications by financial creditors under Section 7 and operational creditors under Section 9.

Guarantor & Security Issues

Matters concerning guarantors, mortgaged property, and the enforcement or release of security interests.

Why Timing and Procedure Decide These Matters

SARFAESI and DRT matters are unforgiving on procedure. A securitisation application has a limitation period; a challenge to a possession notice must be brought in the correct form and forum; and an appeal before the DRAT may require a pre-deposit. Acting early, in the right forum, with the correct grounds, is what preserves a party's position. Where a notice under the SARFAESI Act has been received, the time to seek advice is immediately, not after possession has been taken.

If You Have Received a Notice

A demand notice or possession notice under the SARFAESI Act sets a clock running. The available remedies narrow as the process advances, so the position should be assessed as soon as a notice is received.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the SARFAESI Act?

The SARFAESI Act (the Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002) allows banks and financial institutions to enforce security interests without the intervention of a court, for example by taking possession of mortgaged property. Borrowers have remedies against such action, principally through a securitisation application before the Debt Recovery Tribunal.

What is a securitisation application (SA) before the DRT?

A securitisation application is the remedy available to a borrower or affected party to challenge measures taken by a secured creditor under the SARFAESI Act. It is filed before the Debt Recovery Tribunal, and there are limitation periods that apply, so it should be brought promptly.

What is the difference between Section 7 and Section 9 of the IBC?

Under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, Section 7 allows a financial creditor to initiate insolvency proceedings against a corporate debtor, while Section 9 allows an operational creditor to do so, subject to conditions including the threshold amount and the resolution of any pre-existing dispute. These matters are heard by the National Company Law Tribunal.

I received a notice from the bank under SARFAESI, what should I do?

A SARFAESI notice sets strict timelines running and the available remedies narrow as the process advances. The position, including the grounds for challenge and the correct forum, should be assessed immediately rather than after possession is taken. Contact the office as soon as a notice is received.

Where are DRT matters heard for Andhra Pradesh?

Securitisation and recovery matters for this region are heard before the Debt Recovery Tribunal at Hyderabad, with appeals lying to the Debt Recovery Appellate Tribunal. The firm acts in matters before these forums.

Dealing with a bank or DRT matter?

If a SARFAESI notice has been received, the position is best assessed without delay.
