You've been told you're more bacteria than human. Ten microbes for every one of your cells.
That number came from a back-of-the-envelope estimate in 1972, and it got repeated for more than forty years.
In 2016, researchers in Israel and Canada finally made a careful estimate. About thirty-eight trillion bacteria, mostly in your colon. About thirty trillion human cells, eighty-four percent of them red blood cells.
So not ten to one. Roughly one point three to one.
Wait. It gets better. Bacteria are tiny. All of yours together weigh about two hundred grams. Roughly one mango.
The ratio isn't even fixed. A single trip to the toilet can flush out enough gut bacteria to tip the balance back toward you.
By count, it's close to a tie. By weight, you're overwhelmingly you. Even though...
